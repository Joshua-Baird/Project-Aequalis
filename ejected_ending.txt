You make your choice.

Kicking off from the wall, you sprint toward the storage lockers where the emergency suits are kept.
The ship’s artificial gravity trembles beneath you, pulsing in irregular waves as systems flicker under the failing containment.

You wrench open the nearest locker and drag out a spacesuit, the heavy polymer plates clattering against the metal floor.
The alarms are deafening now—pressure warnings, oxygen loss, temperature fluctuations—all blending into a mechanical howl.

Your data pad buzzes as you seal the chestplate.
--------------------------------------------------
⚠ ATMOSPHERIC INTEGRITY: 11%
⚠ FORCE FIELD FAILURE IMMINENT — 00:45 REMAINING
⚠ RECOMMENDED ACTION: Evacuate Cargo Hold
--------------------------------------------------
You force your arms into the sleeves, locking the seals. The suit’s helmet clamps shut with a hydraulic hiss. Oxygen readings flicker to life on your HUD.

Thirty-five seconds left.

You secure the magnetic boots and grab hold of the nearest railing, preparing to move toward the breach.
Then, with a deep metallic groan, the deck plates shudder beneath your feet.
Your data pad chirps again—its glow flickering erratically.
---------------------------------------------
⚠ WARNING: CARGO EJECTION SEQUENCE INITIATED
⚠ SOURCE: BRIDGE COMMAND
---------------------------------------------
You barely have time to process it.

The shimmering shield at the end of the cargo bay suddenly disappears. The vacuum of space tears through the room like a living thing.
Crates, debris, and loose containers explode outward. The pull rips you off your feet before you can anchor down.

You’re thrown into space.

The ship shrinks rapidly behind you, silhouetted against the curved distortion of the black hole.
You’re weightless—drifting, spinning slowly.

Through the blur of stars, something catches your eye.

The bridge.

You can see it through the viewport.
A lone figure stands there, framed in red emergency light, staring out toward the debris field.
At first, you think it’s someone else. But then you see their face reflected in the glass.

It’s you.

You’re watching yourself drift away.
For a moment, your other self just stands there—still, silent—before turning and walking out of sight.

You continue to spin. Your HUD flares with proximity warnings and oxygen alerts, then begins to fail one by one.
The ship falls behind you as the warped halo of the black hole dominates your view.

The stars twist and vanish into its light.
And then you’re gone.

— End of Part 1 —
