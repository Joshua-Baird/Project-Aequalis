You keep moving, one hand trailing along the wall for balance. The metal is cold now, almost slick with condensation.

The air tastes of ozone. The hum beneath your feet grows louder—deeper—like the heartbeat of something vast and breaking.
You pass through a half-collapsed hatch and emerge into Engineering.

It’s chaos.
The main reactor glows like a wounded sun, arcs of energy crawling across its fractured casing.
Panels spark and die, conduits bleed light. You can feel the static in your teeth.

Someone is already there—
standing at the main control station, back to you, hands moving over the controls with frantic precision.

“What does that matter? We’ll figure it out later. For now—how are we going to restore power?”
