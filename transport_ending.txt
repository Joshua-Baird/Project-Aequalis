You pull up the ship’s schematic, your pulse hammering in your ears.
The Transport Bay isn’t far—two decks down, one corridor across.

You move fast. The bridge doors slide open with a reluctant hiss, and you step into a corridor bathed in the rhythmic pulse of red emergency lights.
Every few meters, sparks rain from an overhead conduit. The ship groans, metal expanding and twisting in protest as the gravity fields waver.

Your footsteps echo down the passage.

As you round the next bend, the light abruptly dies.
Darkness swallows everything ahead.

The emergency panels here are dead—no glow, no hum. Just a void. You can barely make out the bulkheads, faint silhouettes in the gloom.
At the far end of the hall, around another corner, a dull red glow pulses faintly.

The Transport Bay.

Somewhere deep within the ship, a low groan echoes through the metal. 
drawn-out and distant, like the ship itself is under immense strain.

You force yourself forward, hand trailing against the wall to keep your balance. The air here is different—charged, humming faintly with static. 
Each step feels heavier, as if gravity itself is tugging harder.
Clank.
Clank.
Clank.
You reach the corner, the red glow bleeding across the floor like a wound.

Then you see it.

A figure stands inside the transport chamber, silhouetted against the swirling crimson light.
The shape is familiar—too familiar.

Before you can speak, a flash of blue arcs through the air.
Your chest ignites with pain so intense it feels like your entire body has been set ablaze.

You collapse to the deck, the world tilting sideways.
The smell of scorched metal and ozone fills your lungs.

Through fading vision, you look up—
and see your own face staring back at you, eyes wide with horror, a smoking laser gun trembling in your double’s hand.

Your last thought, before darkness takes you, is a single frozen realization:

You just killed yourself.
